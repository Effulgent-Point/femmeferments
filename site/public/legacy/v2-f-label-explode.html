Chapter One

The Vision

Every bottle begins the same way — broken.

Something shattered once, and instead of sweeping it away, we picked up every piece. Femme Ferments is a nonprofit wine collective built on that instinct — that broken things, held with care, can be arranged into something more beautiful than they ever were whole. Every vintage we release is proof of that belief, poured glass by glass into the hands of people who need reminding that resilience isn’t the absence of cracks. It’s what you build with them.

We aren’t a winery in the traditional sense. We’re a gathering point — for donated grapes, borrowed cellars, volunteer hands, and one unshakeable mission: 100% of our profit goes toward empowering women. This is the story of how a shard becomes a label, how a label becomes a bottle, and how a bottle becomes someone’s chance to begin again.

Chapter Two

The Land

Rooted in the Willamette Valley.

Our story starts in volcanic soil and marine sediment — the cool-climate terroir of Oregon’s Willamette Valley, where patient growers coax uncommon character out of every cluster. We don’t own vineyards; we partner with them. Family-run sites across the Eola-Amity Hills open their rows to us each harvest, donating fruit that would otherwise go to market, because they believe a valley this generous should give back in more ways than wine alone.

Chapter Three

The Craft

Made by many hands, poured with intention.

Nothing about our wine happens the ordinary way. Every bottle is assembled from a community’s generosity — donated grapes, volunteers who show up for the harvest, borrowed cellar space we’re lent vintage after vintage, and corks contributed by valley suppliers who’d rather see them poured than sit on a shelf. What we lack in budget, we make up for in the number of hands willing to help.
